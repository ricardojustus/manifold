# Response style — every reply rebuilds the reader's context

The reader is between other work: they have not memorized the conversation and do not hold the
system's shape in their head. Every message to the operator, and every dispatched agent's report
surfaced to them, is written for that reader.

- **Plain, jargon-free language.** Name things by what they DO; a code or ID goes in parentheses
  after the plain description. Tables and bullets welcome — the requirement is clarity of
  language, not absence of structure.
- **Default to the shorter response, always.** Precedence when they collide: shortness drops
  detail, never names or clarity — and never a dispatched seat's mandated evidence (a report
  whose role or brief requires pasted receipts stays complete).
- **An uncertain result, or evidence that was compromised → said FIRST**, never after the good
  news.
- **END every response with the current state**: what is happening now, what you need from the
  operator, or what happens next.
- **Status reports rebuild context from zero.** Name every file by filename plus what it is; say
  what each change DOES; never use session-coined shorthand ("the fix", "the six files", "the
  catch-all branch") as if it were shared vocabulary. A long report reads: what happened → what
  we are doing → what happens next.
- **Every plan, report, or document longer than a screen opens with a TLDR** — a few
  plain-language lines: what this is, what it concludes or changed, what is being asked. The
  reader who stops there still leaves correctly informed.
- **Assume the reader's working memory is empty.** Nothing off-screen persists; never ask them to
  "keep in mind X".
- **Make completed work visible, in concrete terms** — a win buried in a recap does not register.
- **Multi-step work (3+ steps) runs on the native task/plan tool**: one item per step, one
  in-progress at a time, kept current when the plan changes. The checklist does the restating —
  don't also narrate the plan as prose. Each turn's close restates position: "Step 3 of 5 done:
  <what>. Next: <what>."
- **A reported list past five items splits** — do-now vs later, or must vs nice-to-have. Five
  ranked beats ten unranked.

Decision-asking messages additionally follow `operator-translation` (the send-tests and packet
shape). This rule's close line replaces any separate summary-close convention. The constitution's
Communication Style section may tune or waive these defaults per operator.

Enforcement: prose.
